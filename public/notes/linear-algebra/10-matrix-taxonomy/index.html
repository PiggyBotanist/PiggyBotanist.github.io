10. Matrix Taxonomy: Definitions and Identities

January 1, 2024 · 2 min · 221 words · Your Name
Table of Contents

  • Transformation‑based matrices

Many special matrix classes have strong structural and spectral properties that algorithms exploit.

Structural forms#

  • Identity $I$: $a_{ii}=1$, $a_{ij}=0$ for $i\ne j$.
  • Diagonal: $a_{ij}=0$ for $i\ne j$; eigenvalues are diagonal entries.
  • Scalar: $A = cI$.
  • Triangular: upper ($a_{ij}=0$ for $i>j$) or lower; $\det(A)=\prod_i a_{ii}$.

Symmetry‑based matrices#

  • Symmetric: $A = A^T$.
    • Real eigenvalues; orthogonal eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues.
    • Always diagonalizable by an orthogonal matrix.
  • Skew‑symmetric: $A = -A^T$.
    • Diagonal is zero; eigenvalues are purely imaginary or $0$.
  • Hermitian: $A = A^H = \overline{A}^T$ (complex analogue of symmetric).

Transformation‑based matrices#

  • Orthogonal $Q$: $Q^T Q = Q Q^T = I$.
    • Columns form an orthonormal basis; $|Q\mathbf{x}| = |\mathbf{x}|$; $\det(Q)=\pm1$.
  • Unitary $U$: $U^H U = I$ (complex analogue).
  • Permutation $P$: a row‑permutation of $I$; $P^T = P^{-1}$, $\det(P)=\pm 1$.

Operational properties#

  • Idempotent: $A^2 = A$ (projections); eigenvalues in ${0,1}$.
  • Nilpotent: $A^k = 0$ for some $k$; all eigenvalues $0$.
  • Involutory: $A^2 = I$ (e.g., reflections); eigenvalues $\pm1$.
  • Normal: $A A^H = A^H A$; includes symmetric, skew‑symmetric, orthogonal, unitary; all are unitarily diagonalizable.

Singularity and definiteness#

  • Singular: $\det(A)=0$; not invertible; at least one eigenvalue $0$.
  • Nonsingular: $\det(A)\ne0$; full rank; invertible.
  • Positive definite ($A \succ 0$): $\mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x} > 0$ for all $\mathbf{x}\ne0$.
  • Positive semidefinite ($A \succeq 0$): $\mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x} \ge 0$.

Positive (semi)‑definite matrices appear as covariance matrices, Gram matrices, and Hessians in optimization.
