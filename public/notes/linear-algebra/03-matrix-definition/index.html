03. Matrices

A matrix $A \in \mathbb{F}^{m \times n}$ represents a linear transformation $T: \mathbb{F}^n \to \mathbb{F}^m$. In high-dimensional contexts, we view matrices as structured operators rather than mere data grids.

1. Fundamental Definitions#

For $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, the entry in the $i$-th row and $j$-th column is denoted $a_{ij}$ or $[A]_{ij}$.

  • Transpose: $A^T$ where $[A]^T_{ij} = a_{ji}$.
  • Trace: Only for square matrices ($n \times n$), $\text{Tr}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n a_{ii}$.
    • Cyclic Property: $\text{Tr}(ABC) = \text{Tr}(BCA) = \text{Tr}(CAB)$.

2. Matrix Multiplication: Four Perspectives#

Given $C = AB$ where $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times p}$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times n}$:

  1. Inner Product (Entry-wise): $c_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^p a_{ik}b_{kj}$.
  2. Column-wise: The columns of $C$ are linear combinations of columns of $A$: $\mathbf{c}_j = A \mathbf{b}_j$.
  3. Row-wise: The rows of $C$ are linear combinations of rows of $B$: $\mathbf{c}_i^T = \mathbf{a}_i^T B$.
  4. Outer Product Sum: $C$ is the sum of $p$ rank-1 matrices:

$$ C = \sum_{k=1}^p \mathbf{a}_k \mathbf{b}_k^T $$

3. Special Matrix Structures#

3.1. Fundamental Matrices#

  • Identity Matrix ($I_n$): A square matrix where $\delta_{ij} = 1$ if $i=j$ and $0$ otherwise.
    • Neutral element: $AI = IA = A$.
  • Scalar Matrix: A diagonal matrix where all diagonal entries are equal ($cI$).
    • Represents uniform scaling in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
  • Diagonal Matrix: $D = \text{diag}(d_1, \dots, d_n)$ where $d_{ij} = 0$ for $i \neq j$.
  • Elementary Matrix ($E$): Generated by a single row operation on $I$. Left-multiplication $EA$ applies the operation to $A$.

3.2. Symmetry and Hermitian Properties#

Properties related to the transpose ($A^T$) and conjugate transpose ($A^H$):

  • Symmetric: $A = A^T$.
  • Hermitian: $A = A^H$ (where $A^H = \overline{A}^T$).
    • For Hermitian matrices, $\langle A\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \rangle = \langle \mathbf{x}, A\mathbf{y} \rangle$.
    • All diagonal entries of a Hermitian matrix must be real.
  • Skew-Hermitian: $A = -A^H$.
  • Normal: $AA^H = A^H A$. Both Hermitian and Unitary matrices are normal.

3.3. Elementary Operations#

Used to manipulate matrices without altering the row space:

  1. Replacement: $R_i \leftarrow R_i + cR_j$.
  2. Interchange: $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$.
  3. Scaling: $R_i \leftarrow cR_i$ ($c \neq 0$).

3.4. Circular Operations and Circulant Matrices#

Indices $i, j$ are treated under modular arithmetic $\pmod n$.

  • Circular Add/Sub: $i \oplus j = (i + j) \pmod n$ and $i \ominus j = (i - j) \pmod n$.
  • Circulant Matrix:

$$ C = \begin{bmatrix} c_0 & c_{n-1} & \dots & c_1 \\ c_1 & c_0 & \dots & c_2 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ c_{n-1} & c_{n-2} & \dots & c_0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad C_{ij} = c_{(i-j) \pmod n} $$

*All circulant matrices commute and are diagonalized by the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) matrix.*

3.5. Structural Classes#

  • Orthogonal/Unitary: $Q^T Q = I$ / $U^H U = I$.
  • Idempotent: $A^2 = A$ (Projection operator).
  • Nilpotent: $A^k = \mathbf{0}$ for some $k$.
  • Permutation ($P$): $P^T P = I$. Rows are a reordering of $I$.

3.6. Block Matrices (Partitioned Matrices)#

Matrices can be subdivided into blocks to simplify operations:

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} \\ A_{21} & A_{22} \end{bmatrix} $$

  • Block Multiplication: Possible if sub-matrix dimensions are compatible.
  • Block Diagonal: $A = \text{diag}(A_1, A_2, \dots, A_k)$.
  • Determinant: For a block diagonal matrix, $\det(A) = \prod \det(A_i)$.

5. Algebraic Properties#

  • Non-commutativity: Generally, $AB \neq BA$.
  • Distributivity: $A(B+C) = AB + AC$.
  • Transpose of Product: $(AB)^T = B^T A^T$.

Practice#

  1. Trace-Inner Product: Show that the Frobenius inner product $\langle A, B \rangle_F = \text{Tr}(A^T B)$ can be written as $\text{vec}(A) \cdot \text{vec}(B)$.
  2. Commutators: The commutator is defined as $[A, B] = AB - BA$. Show that if $A$ and $B$ are skew-symmetric, their commutator is also skew-symmetric.
  3. Orthogonal Matrices: Prove that if $Q$ is an orthogonal matrix, then $\det(Q) = \pm 1$.
  4. Rank-1 Update: Let $A$ be a square matrix and $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$ be vectors. Express the trace of the updated matrix $A + \mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T$ in terms of $\text{Tr}(A)$ and $\mathbf{u}^T \mathbf{v}$.
