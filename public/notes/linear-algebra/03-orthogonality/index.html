03. Orthogonality

January 1, 2024 · 1 min · 132 words · Your Name
Table of Contents
  • Definitions
  • Key properties

Orthogonality generalizes “perpendicular” and leads to orthonormal bases and matrices that preserve lengths and angles—essential for stable numerics and many algorithms.

Definitions#

  • Orthogonal vectors: $\mathbf{u} \perp \mathbf{v}$ if $\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0$.
  • Orthogonal set: Every pair of vectors in the set is orthogonal.
  • Orthonormal set: An orthogonal set of unit vectors: $\mathbf{q}_i \cdot \mathbf{q}j = \delta{ij}$ (Kronecker delta).

Key properties#

Pythagorean theorem: $\mathbf{u} \perp \mathbf{v} \iff |\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}|^2 = |\mathbf{u}|^2 + |\mathbf{v}|^2$.

Orthogonal matrix $Q$: A square matrix with $Q^T Q = I$ (and hence $Q Q^T = I$). Equivalently:

  • Columns (and rows) form an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$.
  • $Q^{-1} = Q^T$.
  • Isometry: $|Q\mathbf{x}| = |\mathbf{x}|$ for all $\mathbf{x}$ (preserves lengths and angles).

Orthogonal matrices are used in QR factorization, eigenvalue algorithms, and anywhere we want to change coordinates without distorting geometry.
