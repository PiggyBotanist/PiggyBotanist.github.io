Gram–Schmidt Process and QR

January 1, 2024 · 1 min · 101 words · Your Name
Table of Contents
  • Gram–Schmidt algorithm
  • Connection to QR

Gram–Schmidt turns a basis into an orthonormal basis; QR factorization is the matrix form of this process.

Gram–Schmidt algorithm#

Given linearly independent ${\mathbf{v}_1,\dots,\mathbf{v}_n}$:

  1. Set $\mathbf{u}_1 = \mathbf{v}_1$.
  2. For $k \ge 2$: $$ \mathbf{u}_k = \mathbf{v}k - \sum{j=1}^{k-1} \frac{\langle \mathbf{v}_k,\mathbf{u}_j\rangle}{\langle \mathbf{u}_j,\mathbf{u}_j\rangle},\mathbf{u}_j. $$
  3. Normalize: $\mathbf{e}_k = \mathbf{u}_k / |\mathbf{u}_k|$.

The $\mathbf{e}_k$ form an orthonormal basis.

Connection to QR#

If $A$ has columns $\mathbf{v}_1,\dots,\mathbf{v}_n$ and Gram–Schmidt produces $\mathbf{e}_1,\dots,\mathbf{e}_n$, define

$$ Q = [\mathbf{e}_1\ \cdots\ \mathbf{e}n], \qquad R{ij} = \langle \mathbf{v}_j,\mathbf{e}_i\rangle. $$

Then $A = QR$ with $Q$ orthogonal and $R$ upper triangular. This is the classical (but not the most numerically stable) QR algorithm.
