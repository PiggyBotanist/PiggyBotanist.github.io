15. Linear Transformations

January 1, 2024 · 2 min · 236 words · Your Name

Linear transformations preserve vector addition and scalar multiplication; in coordinates they are exactly matrix multiplications.

Definition#

$T : V \to W$ is linear if for all $\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v} \in V$ and scalars $c$:

  1. $T(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}) = T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v})$,
  2. $T(c\mathbf{u}) = c,T(\mathbf{u})$.

Equivalently, $T(c\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}) = cT(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v})$.

Standard matrix#

For $T : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$, there exists a unique matrix $A$ such that $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$. If $\mathbf{e}_i$ are the standard basis vectors,

$$ A = \big[ T(\mathbf{e}_1)\ \cdots\ T(\mathbf{e}_n) \big]. $$

Kernel and image#

  • $\ker(T) = {\mathbf{x} : T(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{0}}$ (null space).
  • $\text{Im}(T) = {T(\mathbf{x}) : \mathbf{x} \in V}$ (range).

They satisfy $\dim(\ker T) + \dim(\text{Im }T) = \dim(V)$ (rank–nullity).

One‑to‑one, onto, and isomorphisms#

  • $T$ is one‑to‑one iff $T(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{0}$ only for $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$ ($\ker T = {0}$).
  • $T$ is onto iff $\text{Im}(T)=W$.
  • If $T$ is both, it is an isomorphism and its matrix is invertible (when $V=W$).

Geometric examples in $\mathbb{R}^2$#

Some standard matrices:

  • Rotation by $\theta$: $$ \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}. $$
  • Reflection across x‑axis: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$.
  • Scaling by $k$: $\begin{bmatrix} k & 0 \\ 0 & k \end{bmatrix}$.
  • Projection onto x‑axis: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

Similarity#

If $A$ and $B$ represent the same linear operator in different bases, they are similar:

$$ B = P^{-1} A P. $$

Similar matrices share determinant, trace, eigenvalues, and characteristic polynomial.\n
