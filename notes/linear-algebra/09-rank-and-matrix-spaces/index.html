09. Rank and the Matrix Spaces

January 1, 2024 · 1 min · 142 words · Your Name
Table of Contents
  • Four fundamental subspaces
  • Full rank vs rank‑deficient
  • Rank identities

For $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, four fundamental subspaces summarize its action and solvability properties.

Four fundamental subspaces#

  • Column space $\text{Col}(A) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$: all $A\mathbf{x}$; dimension $r = \text{rank}(A)$.
  • Row space $\text{Row}(A) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$: span of rows; dimension $r$.
  • Null space $\text{Nul}(A) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$: all $\mathbf{x}$ with $A\mathbf{x}=0$; dimension $n-r$.
  • Left null space $\text{Nul}(A^T) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$: all $\mathbf{y}$ with $A^T\mathbf{y}=0$; dimension $m-r$.

Rank–nullity theorem:

$$ \text{rank}(A) + \dim(\text{Nul}(A)) = n. $$

Full rank vs rank‑deficient#

  • Full rank: $\text{rank}(A) = \min(m,n)$.
  • Full column rank: $\text{rank}(A) = n$; $\text{Nul}(A) = {\mathbf{0}}$.
  • Full row rank: $\text{rank}(A) = m$; $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ is solvable for all $\mathbf{b}$.
  • Rank‑deficient: $\text{rank}(A) < \min(m,n)$; if $m=n$, $A$ is singular.

Rank identities#

  • $\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}(A^T) = \text{rank}(A^T A) = \text{rank}(A A^T)$.
  • $\text{rank}(AB) \le \min(\text{rank}(A), \text{rank}(B))$.

Understanding rank and these spaces is essential for solving $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$, least squares, and factorization theory (e.g., SVD).
