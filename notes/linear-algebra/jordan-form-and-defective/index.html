Jordan Normal Form and Defective Matrices

January 1, 2024 · 1 min · 167 words · Your Name
Table of Contents
  • Generalized eigenvectors
  • Jordan blocks and Jordan matrix

When $A$ does not have enough eigenvectors to be diagonalizable, Jordan form provides a nearly diagonal structure using generalized eigenvectors.

Generalized eigenvectors#

For eigenvalue $\lambda$, a nonzero $\mathbf{v}$ is a generalized eigenvector of rank $k$ if

$$ (A - \lambda I)^k \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0} \quad \text{but} \quad (A - \lambda I)^{k-1} \mathbf{v} \ne \mathbf{0}. $$

Rank‑1 generalized eigenvectors are the usual eigenvectors.

Jordan blocks and Jordan matrix#

A $k\times k$ Jordan block is

$$ J_k(\lambda) = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda & 1 & & 0 \\ & \lambda & \ddots & \\ & & \ddots & 1 \\ 0 & & & \lambda \end{bmatrix}. $$

Every square matrix $A$ over $\mathbb{C}$ is similar to a block diagonal matrix $J$ of Jordan blocks:

$$ A = M J M^{-1}. $$

For each eigenvalue:

  • The algebraic multiplicity is the sum of the sizes of its blocks.
  • The geometric multiplicity is the number of blocks.

If every block has size 1, $J$ is diagonal and $A$ is diagonalizable; otherwise $A$ is defective.
