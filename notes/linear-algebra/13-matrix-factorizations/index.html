13. Matrix Factorizations

January 1, 2024 · 1 min · 200 words · Your Name
Table of Contents

Matrix factorizations rewrite matrices as products with simpler structure, making systems and least squares problems faster and more stable to solve.

LU and PLU#

If $A$ can be reduced without row swaps:

$$ A = LU, $$

with $L$ unit lower triangular and $U$ upper triangular.

In general,

$$ P A = L U, $$

where $P$ is a permutation matrix encoding row swaps.

Solving $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ becomes solve $L\mathbf{y} = P\mathbf{b}$ (forward) then $U\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{y}$ (backward).

LDU#

$A = L D U$ with $L,U$ unit triangular and $D$ diagonal of pivots; unique when $A$ is invertible and LU exists.

Cholesky#

For symmetric positive definite $A$,

$$ A = L L^T = R^T R, $$

with $L$ lower triangular, $l_{ii} > 0$. Cholesky is cheaper than LU and common for covariance matrices and SPD systems.

QR#

For $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ full column rank:

$$ A = Q R, $$

where $Q$ has orthonormal columns and $R$ is upper triangular. Least squares uses $R\hat{\mathbf{x}} = Q^T \mathbf{b}$.

Spectral (for symmetric $A$)#

$$ A = Q \Lambda Q^T = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i \mathbf{q}_i \mathbf{q}_i^T. $$

This expresses $A$ as a sum of rank‑1 projections; related to PCA and quadratic forms.\n
